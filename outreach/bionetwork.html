How this connects to our research

Smart biomaterials need to sense their environment to respond appropriately — a wound dressing that detects infection and releases antibiotics only when needed requires accurate sensing of chemical signals like pH, glucose, or bacterial toxins.

This game captures a core engineering challenge: how do you get complete environmental coverage with minimal sensor resources? The accuracy vs. cost tradeoff is real — implanted biosensors must be small, biocompatible, and power-efficient, making optimal placement critical.

Welcome to Biological Network Sense!

Design and manage a network of biological sensors to accurately read environmental values at specific target concentration zones, while managing energy costs.

How to Play:

  • Add Sensors: Click anywhere on the canvas to place a new sensor. Use the radio buttons to select the sensor type.
  • Remove Sensors: Right-click on a sensor to remove it.
  • Target Concentration Zones & I/O Readers: These will appear automatically on the canvas. Your goal is to wire sensors to the I/O Readers to read the target values. Right-click a target zone to remove both.
  • Wire Sensors: Left-click a sensor to select it (it will glow cyan). Then, left-click an I/O Reader to connect/disconnect the selected sensor to/from that reader. **Note: Sensors can only connect to I/O Readers within their connection range.**
  • Toggle Insulation: Select a sensor, then click "Toggle Insulation" to make it less affected by environmental stressors and *more* affected by connected sensors. Insulated sensors have a dashed border.
  • Biological Medium: The background shows a gradient of biological concentration with persistent concentration fields (hot/cold spots). Environmental stressors (anomalies) will appear randomly.
  • Energy & Budget: Track your total energy cost, which increases with sensor/I/O reader placement and recurring maintenance. Manage your energy budget with the slider.
  • Network Redundancy: This measures how many sensors are covering the same area. Higher redundancy means your network is more robust against sensor failures and environmental noise, but it also increases cost.
  • Win/Loss: Achieve high network accuracy and redundancy to win, or run out of energy/fail to meet performance targets to lose!
